# Direction sheet and visual review

Write a short direction before editing. For example, an independent cinema announcement might use an off-center poster composition, warm paper against near-black, a restrained signal-red accent, an assertive title face and quieter supporting type. These are choices for that brief, not a default palette for every video. Confirm the uploaded artwork can express them before promising the result.

| Decision | Make it concrete | Inspect it |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy | Name the headline, supporting fact and action. Give each a distinct size/weight role within source artwork. | In a small preview, identify the headline without reading everything. Does the action compete too early? |
| Typography | Prefer a small set of compatible type roles with deliberate contrast; keep case, punctuation and baseline alignment consistent. | Check actual glyphs, line breaks and counters in the uploaded art. Look at thin strokes after compression. A font name alone proves nothing. |
| Grid/spacing | Choose a common text edge, a shared baseline or a deliberate asymmetry. Repeat a spacing unit rather than scattering unrelated gaps. | Scrub the whole movement: no title, logo or supporting line should hit a margin accidentally. |
| Color | Assign background, primary text, secondary text and accent roles. Reserve the strongest accent for meaningful emphasis. | Review grayscale and low-resolution frames when available. Moving content behind a title must not erase its contrast. |
| Detail | Give each rule, number, texture or ornament an editorial purpose. | Remove an element mentally: if the message becomes clearer and the identity survives, consider removing it in the next authorized edit. |
| Brand | Match tone, logo clear space and authorized colors/type. | Distinguish a requested departure from an accidental inconsistency. Do not invent asset licenses or substitute a logo. |

At delivery size, ask whether the hierarchy survives a quick glance, whether each line can be read during its hold, and whether the frame belongs to the stated audience. A detailed desktop layout can fail on a phone. Inspect actual exported frames because chroma subsampling and scaling can soften small colored text.

For a multi-scene piece, reuse a visual relationship across cuts: one alignment, an accent location or a direction of movement. Change enough to signal a new idea without rebuilding the visual language each time. Static transparency in a PNG can provide a silhouette or occlusion layer; it does not provide an editable mask tool.

Review feedback should name a visible defect, its time range and a supported correction: “The secondary line intersects the orbit at 1.2 seconds; reduce the orbit's scale or shift its X track,” rather than “make it more premium.” Do not revise a user's approved artwork solely to satisfy a generic style rule.
